## Core Thesis

Tome I does not yet claim a finished physical model. It develops the claim that observables should be reconstructed as stable reductions of a deeper entity rather than postulated as independent primitive objects. The central object is provisionally denoted `\mathfrak{S}`; its later compressed kernel is denoted `\mathfrak{K}_{min}`.

## Five Ontological Theses

- **Primacy of structure:** particles, fields, and geometry are not primitive things; they are stable organizations inside a deeper structure of admissible states, transitions, and constraints.
- **Admissibility over locality:** global compatibility with topology, spectrum, spin structure, boundary conditions, and holonomy is deeper than local constructibility.
- **Multilayer reality:** the same underlying content can be read geometrically, spectrally, topological-phase-wise, correlationally, and as effective physical observables.
- **Particles and interactions as modes:** particles are stable modes, charge is an oriented phase branch, and mass is structural inertia of a regime.
- **Single source:** RPFT, UGSM, TOE-like correlation language, and physical observables should be treated as reductions of one underlying entity, not as unrelated theories.

## Three Research Languages

- **Geometric language:** compact carriers, volumes, curvature, topology, and invariant spaces.
- **Spectral language:** operators, eigenmodes, multiplicities, heat-kernel asymptotics, zeta residues, and stability under deformations.
- **Correlational language:** global relations and nonlocal consistency patterns that may not be visible in a purely local/geometric representation.

Tome I argues that none of the three languages is sufficient alone. A serious theory must explain why the same structure survives across them.

## Five Reconstruction Phases

- **Invariant kernel:** isolate what remains stable across descriptions and deformations.
- **Minimal formalization:** introduce the smallest object capable of carrying that invariant content.
- **Derive reductions:** show how geometric, spectral, correlational, and effective languages arise from the same object.
- **Back-check prior material:** verify that old successes are reproduced without hidden parameters.
- **New predictive distinctions:** generate fresh tests that were not used to build the framework.

This is the methodological precursor of Tome II's Definition of Done and audit/no-go structure.

## Formal Postulates

- **Admissibility postulate:** only globally consistent configurations count as physically meaningful candidates.
- **Operator generativity postulate:** observable structure must be obtainable through operator readings of the underlying entity.
- **Geometric realizability postulate:** the entity must admit geometric realizations, not only abstract symbolic descriptions.
- **Correlational wholeness postulate:** reductions must remain mutually compatible as shadows of one entity.
- **Invariant-kernel postulate:** there must be a stable core that survives allowed deformations and changes of representation.

## Trial Realization of `\mathfrak{S}`

Tome I sketches `\mathfrak{S}` as a carrier of admissible states, reductions, operators, and correlational constraints. It is decomposed into sectors:

- **Support-frame sector:** geometric/topological backbone and leading invariants.
- **Phase sector:** holonomies, branch choices, spin/fundamental-group information.
- **Internal-load sector:** richer operator/internal structures that can shift subleading spectra or effective parameters.
- **Mixed sector:** cross-terms where observables cannot be assigned to one sector alone.

## Observable Attribution

Tome I proposes that observables should be classified by dominant regime:

- **Frame-dominated:** controlled mostly by geometric or topological carrier data.
- **Phase-dominated:** sensitive to global branches, holonomy, and spin/phase choices.
- **Internal-load-dominated:** controlled by richer internal operator content.
- **Mixed:** requires cross-sector terms or interference between sectors.
- **Transitional:** changes class under deformation or near bifurcation boundaries.

This map is important for Tome II because `S_geo`, `S_vac`, lepton masses, neutrino insertions, and EW/QCD thresholds should not all be treated as the same kind of observable.

## Model Selection Criteria

Tome I introduces model-selection rules that later become no-go/audit discipline:

- **Regime compatibility:** a model must place each observable in the right kind of regime.
- **Structural economy:** it should not add entities or coefficients that do no explanatory work.
- **Stability under deformations:** results should survive allowed deformations and parameterization changes.
- **Inter-language portability:** claims should be readable in more than one language when appropriate.
- **Predictive selection:** the model should create new distinctions, not only fit already-known numbers.

## Minimal Kernel

Tome I compresses the framework toward:

```text
K_min = (S, I(S), R_type, Q_sel)
```

where:

- `S` is the underlying entity.
- `I(S)` is the invariant kernel.
- `R_type` is the minimal typology of reduction/reading regimes.
- `Q_sel` is the model-selection principle.

Tome II translates this into a technical surrogate:

```text
(K, Spec, D_train, L_EFT, Q_audit)
```

This translation is useful but not identical: Tome II operationalizes the kernel for spectral closure, while the full reconstruction of `S` remains open.

## Reduction Compression

Tome I distinguishes three statuses for framework elements:

- **Fundamental:** cannot be removed without losing identity, regime distinction, or model selection.
- **Derivative:** recoverable from the kernel as a coordinate system, expansion, or local representation.
- **Borderline:** plausibly derivative but not yet reconstructed rigorously.

Important borderline/derivative questions include observable classes, local model hierarchies, and regime maps.

## Almost-Axiomatic Kernel

Later sections introduce a near-axiomatic skeleton:

- axiom of unified entity;
- axiom of invariant identity;
- axiom of regime distinguishability;
- axiom of nontrivial selection;
- lemmas on preservation under weak deformations and reductions;
- a theorem-schema stating that the current apparatus can distinguish fundamental, derivative, and borderline layers.

## Dynamics and Quasi-Action

Tome I then sketches dynamics on the minimal kernel:

- a space of admissible realizations;
- weak dynamics that preserve the invariant kernel;
- strong rupture when the invariant kernel or regime typology changes;
- a quasigradient principle favoring higher selection quality;
- a quasi-action with invariant, regime, and selection contributions.

This is not yet a physical action in the Tome II sense. It is a proto-dynamic selection framework.

## Status at End of Tome I

Tome I closes as a conceptual and methodological foundation, not as a final theory. It establishes:

- a candidate ontology;
- a reconstruction protocol;
- a sector/regime language;
- a model-selection discipline;
- a minimal kernel candidate;
- a proto-dynamics on the kernel.

It explicitly requires the next stage to replace qualitative structure with operators, spectra, compact carriers, calculable functionals, reproducible audits, and no-go criteria. This is exactly the task taken up in Tome II.

## Relation to Tome II

- Tome II implements admissibility via allowed inputs, topology, spin structures, holonomies, and determinant schemes.

- Tome II implements model selection via blind tests, ablation, permutation controls, and no-go criteria.
- Tome II only partially implements correlational wholeness: cross-sector links exist, but full reconstruction of `\mathfrak{S}` is still II.B work.

## Open Questions

- Can `\mathfrak{S}` be reconstructed as an actual operator/geometric/correlational object rather than a methodological placeholder?
- Can every observable be assigned to a regime class without post-hoc relabeling?
- Can the quasi-action become a genuine variational principle or path-integral object?
- Can the Tome II spectral carrier `RP^3 x S^1` be derived from the Tome I kernel rather than selected by minimal closure tests?
- Can cross-sector correlational wholeness be demonstrated for EM, leptons, neutrinos, Higgs/EW, and QCD simultaneously?
